Final project

Write the case

Forty per cent of the grade, in groups of four. The requirement is access, not fame.

What you are producing

A teaching case your group sources itself, from somebody's own world. A decision one of you watched, a company one of you worked at, an industry one of you has access to. It stops at the decision point, exactly as every case in this course does.

The requirement is access, not fame. An obscure decision with real documents beats a famous one with only news coverage. The failure mode this brief exists to prevent is a group picking a fascinating story, finding no primary material, and submitting a summary of news articles in December.

Four members means four possible sources of material, which is the main reason this is a group assignment rather than an individual one. The solo version fails when a student has a great idea and no way in. A group picks whichever member has the best access.

Deliverables

  • The case. Twelve to fifteen pages, stopping at the decision point.
  • Exhibits. At least four, of which at least two are numeric, drawn from material you actually obtained.
  • A teaching note. Every role, the discussion questions, and what the instructor should know that the room should not, including the outcome.
  • The presentation. Twelve minutes in week 14 plus five of questions, with every member answering.

Named ownership

Each of the four signs a component: the narrative, the exhibits, the teaching note, the presentation. The signature appears in the document. This does not prevent free riding on its own, but combined with live questions to every member in week 14 it makes the shape of each contribution visible.

Peer evaluation

A confidential form at submission, four short questions, applied as a multiplier on the group mark rather than scored as its own component. It exists, you are being told in week 1 that it exists, and that is most of what it is for.

Groups form earlier than feels necessary because the week 5 pitch requires you to have already found a story and confirmed access, and that takes two weeks of asking around.

The week 5 checkpoint is about access, not topic

The pitch has to name the documents you can actually obtain and the people who will actually talk to you. A pitch that cannot name them is rejected, and rejected early is the point: a group that discovers in December that the material does not exist has nothing to submit.

Being working professionals, your access is unusually good, and the constraint flips. You can get real material, and much of it belongs to your employer. Read the confidentiality rules below before you start reporting, not after.

Confidentiality

You need this rule before you make the first phone call.

Anonymise by default
"A mid-size health insurer" carries the case as well as the name does. The teaching value is in the decision, not the logo.
Named employers need written permission
From somebody with the authority to give it. One email, reproduced in the appendix.
Nothing under NDA, ever
Anonymised or not. Anonymisation is not a legal defence and you should not treat it as one.
Sessions are recorded
Which matters most before the week 5 pitches, because something said in a pitch cannot be unsaid.

The version of this constraint worth wanting: a case built entirely from documents you can lawfully share is a case that can be taught next year. The ones that cannot be shared die with this cohort.

What happens to the good ones

The strongest three or four cases each year become teaching material the following year, with the student authors credited. After two cohorts this course is substantially built from cases its own students wrote.

This project is graded on whether the frames from the twelve case weeks transferred to material nobody prepared for you.
