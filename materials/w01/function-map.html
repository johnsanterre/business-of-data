The twelve functions

What each one owns, and what it cannot see from where it sits.

Twelve people sit around a table at one company. Each week of this course you take one of their roles. Every function is defined by two things: what it is accountable for, and what it cannot observe from its position.

The second column is the one that matters. A panel of students holding four of these roles produces an argument rather than a consensus because the roles genuinely cannot see the same things. Almost every failure in this course happens in a blind spot rather than in a decision.

How a release happens

A capability becomes a product by passing a sequence of gates. Internal evaluation, red teaming, legal review, a pricing decision, a launch date chosen by somebody with a calendar, and a communications plan. At each gate, note who signs off, who can stop it, and who only finds out afterwards.

The buyer has a parallel sequence, and half the cases in this course turn on it: pilot, security review, legal, procurement, deployment. It takes nine months.

The line worth carrying out of week one is that the product manager owns the launch date and has authority over nobody. Every function above can slow a release. The person accountable for it can only ask.

Three shapes of failure

Business failures are almost never one bad decision. They are a decision made correctly inside one function's frame that is catastrophic in another's, with nobody owning the seam between them. Ask which of these three shapes a case fits, every week.

The unfilled role
Nobody is sitting in the seat. The function exists on the organisation chart and not in the building, or it is occupied by somebody with no authority to stop anything.
The seam
Two functions each optimising correctly and in good faith, with the failure living in the space between their mandates, which appears on neither scorecard.
The disclosure
The act of reporting the problem is what triggers the collapse. Telling the truth on Wednesday afternoon is what makes Thursday happen, and the alternatives were worse, or illegal, or both.

By week 13 you will have seen all three in the same company on the same weekend.

Holding a role

The panel format asks you to defend what the real person actually did, with what they knew on that date. Not what you would do knowing the ending. Hindsight makes every decision in this course look obvious, and a student who is permitted to say what they would have done gets to posture instead of reconstructing the constraints.

Conceding a good point scores. A panel that never yields is a panel that is not listening.
