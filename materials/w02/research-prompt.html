Research prompt

The prompt behind both reports

One prompt, given to two systems, producing two reports.

Two background research reports are assigned this week. Both were produced from the prompt below, without modification. One was written by Claude and one by Google Gemini Deep Research. Neither was edited afterwards to agree with the other.

Read both, and read them against each other. The instruction to state whether each claim is filed, reported, or not established was given to both systems, and how each one handled that instruction is worth as much attention as the content.

The prompt, as issued

Produce an extensive, source-cited case study of CoreWeave suitable for teaching
in a graduate business course. The teaching question is how a company decides
what its assets are worth on the books, and what follows from that decision.
Freeze the narrative at the initial public offering on 28 March 2025. Material
after that date belongs in a clearly separated final section.

Work only from primary documents where they exist: the Form S-1 and its
amendments, the Form 424B4 prospectus, subsequent 10-Q and 10-K filings, credit
agreements filed as exhibits, and the annual filings of the comparator companies
named below. Where you rely on journalism or analyst work, name the outlet, the
author, and the date, and mark the claim as reported rather than filed. Where a
figure is disputed, give both figures and say who asserts each. Do not smooth
over gaps: if something is not disclosed, say that it is not disclosed.

Cover the following.

1. Origin and conversion. Atlantic Crypto, the founders, the Ethereum merge in
   2022, and the decision to convert mining hardware and operational knowledge
   into a cloud business. What was retained and what had to be rebuilt.

2. The financial record through 2024. Revenue, cost of revenue, gross margin,
   operating loss, net loss, adjusted measures and exactly which items the
   adjustments remove. Property and equipment at gross cost, accumulated
   depreciation, and net carrying value. Capital expenditure by year.

3. The depreciation policy in full. The stated method and useful life for
   technology equipment, the exact language in the accounting policies note, any
   residual value assumption, whether the estimate has ever been revised, and
   what the auditor identified as a critical audit matter if anything.

4. Peer comparison of useful life for servers and data centre equipment:
   Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Oracle, Nebius, Applied Digital, Crusoe,
   and Lambda where disclosed. For each, give the current stated life, the prior
   life, the date of any change, and the quantified earnings effect the company
   disclosed for the change. Pay particular attention to Amazon shortening
   certain server lives from six years to five effective 1 January 2025, and
   establish what reasoning Amazon gave.

5. Evidence on the economic life of data centre GPUs. Assemble what is actually
   known rather than asserted: resale and secondary market pricing for A100 and
   H100 class hardware over time, rental price decay by generation, utilisation
   rates, failure and retirement rates, NVIDIA's architecture release cadence,
   and any operator that has published retirement data. Distinguish physical
   life, economic life, and accounting life throughout.

6. The debt structure. Every facility by name, size, date, rate, maturity, and
   amortisation profile. The collateral package and how the borrowing base is
   computed. Covenants, including which are computed from figures that
   depreciation affects. Lease and power commitments that function as
   liabilities without appearing as debt.

7. Customer concentration. Microsoft at 62 per cent of 2024 revenue and the top
   two at 77 per cent. Identify the second customer if it is identifiable from
   any filing. Contract terms, duration, take-or-pay provisions, and termination
   rights to the extent disclosed. The relationship between Microsoft as
   customer, NVIDIA as supplier and investor, and CoreWeave.

8. The offering itself. Timing, pricing against the initial range, the size
   reduction if any, the anchor investors, how the book was received, and what
   the underwriters and press said about demand.

9. The bear case as it stood in March 2025, constructed only from what was
   knowable then. Which assumptions carry the most weight with the least
   support, and what would falsify each one.

10. A separate final section, clearly labelled as after the decision point:
    share price history since listing, subsequent filings, later contracts, the
    Core Scientific transaction, published short theses including Kerrisdale, the
    November 2025 argument by Michael Burry that hyperscalers understate
    depreciation, and any revision CoreWeave has made to its useful life
    estimate.

Deliver as a structured report with numbered sections, a table of every
quantitative claim with its source and filing date, and a bibliography. Flag
explicitly any place where the public record is thin enough that a reader should
not rely on it.

What to compare

Read the two reports side by side and answer four questions.

  1. Where do they disagree on a figure, and which one is closer to the filing?
  2. Where does one report a claim as established that the other marks as unverified, and which handling is right?
  3. What did each one leave out that the prompt asked for?
  4. Which claims appear in both and are wrong in both? These are the most dangerous, because agreement between two systems reads as corroboration and is not.
