Background research

CoreWeave: a chronology

What happened, when, and on whose authority. 2017 to September 2026.

This is a record of events, not a case. The case packet is a separate document and stops at March 2025. This chronology runs to the present and is for the instructor and for students assigned a role.

Claims are marked filed when they come from a document lodged with a regulator, reported when they come from journalism or analyst work, and not established where this research did not settle the question. Section 8 lists the status of every quantitative claim. Where a figure is disputed, both versions appear.

1. 2017 to 2019: mining

2017. Reported. Michael Intrator, Brian Venturo, and Brannin McBee found Atlantic Crypto. All three come from energy commodities trading. Intrator and Venturo had worked together at Hudson Ridge Asset Management. The business mines Ethereum on NVIDIA graphics processors, beginning with a single card on a pool table in a Wall Street office and expanding into a converted garage in New Jersey.

2018. Reported. Cryptocurrency prices fall and competing miners liquidate. Atlantic Crypto buys hardware from distressed sellers at a discount rather than contracting, and begins renting spare capacity to other businesses.

2019. Reported. The company renames itself CoreWeave and begins pursuing workloads beyond mining. What it carries out of the mining business is the hardware, the power and cooling relationships, and the operational practice of running large GPU fleets at high utilisation.

2. 2020 to 2022: the conversion, and the merge

2020 to 2022. Reported. The company builds the apparatus a cloud provider needs and a miner does not: scheduling, multi-tenancy, networking, contracts, security review, and enterprise sales.

September 2022. Public record. The Ethereum merge moves the network from proof of work to proof of stake and ends GPU mining on that chain. The original business no longer exists. The conversion was already underway, which matters to how the company is read: it had changed direction before the change was forced.

Full year 2022. Filed. Revenue of $16 million.

3. 2023: the financing begins, and the estimate changes

2023. Reported. The most consequential item in this chronology. CoreWeave changes its useful life estimate for technology equipment from five years to six years. At the fleet sizes involved the change reduces annual depreciation expense by a reported several hundred million dollars. It occurs two years before the company sells shares to the public and during the period in which it is borrowing heavily against the same equipment.

2023. Filed. Alphabet extends the estimated useful life of servers from four years to six, and of certain network equipment from five years to six, effective in fiscal 2023. Oracle extends to five years. Microsoft had already moved from four years to six in 2022. The direction of travel across the industry is toward longer lives, and each change raises reported operating income in the year it is made.

August 2023. Reported. CoreWeave secures a $2.3 billion debt facility led by Magnetar Capital and Blackstone, collateralised by equipment and contracted customer receivables.

Full year 2023. Filed. Revenue of $229 million, growth of 1,346 per cent. Remaining performance obligations of $9.9 billion at 31 December.

4. 2024: scale, and the first approach to Core Scientific

2024. Reported. A $7.5 billion facility follows, again led by Blackstone and Magnetar. Not established: the precise closing date and the facility's pricing.

June 2024. Reported. Core Scientific rejects an all-cash acquisition offer from CoreWeave.

Through 31 December 2024. Filed. Total debt commitments raised reach $12.9 billion. Facilities named in the later prospectus include the 2024 Term Loan Facility and the DDTL 2.0 Facility.

Full year 2024. Filed. Revenue of $1.9 billion, growth of 737 per cent. Net loss of $863 million. Adjusted net loss of $65 million. The roughly $800 million difference removes stock-based compensation and certain financing items, and does not remove depreciation.

Full year 2024. Filed. Microsoft accounts for 62 per cent of revenue. The top two customers together account for 77 per cent. Reported: the second is NVIDIA, which is simultaneously the principal supplier, an equity investor, and a customer.

31 December 2024. Filed. Remaining performance obligations of $15.1 billion, up 53 per cent from $9.9 billion.

Not established. Gross margin, capital expenditure, gross cost of property and equipment, accumulated depreciation, and net carrying value for 2024. All are in the financial statements and should be retrieved.

5. January to March 2025: two disclosures, six weeks apart

Effective 1 January 2025. Filed. Amazon shortens the useful life of certain servers and networking equipment from six years to five. It is the only large operator in this period to move in that direction. Not established: the reasoning Amazon gave, which is disclosed and should be retrieved.

Effective 1 January 2025. Filed. Meta extends the useful life of certain servers and network assets to 5.5 years, moving the other way.

March 2025. Filed. CoreWeave files its registration statement and amendments. The accounting policies note states that technology equipment is depreciated straight-line over six years. The filing discloses the customer concentration, the debt, and the accounting policy. Reported: the initial price range is $47 to $55 on 49 million shares.

27 March 2025. Reported. Demand fails to materialise at the range. Contemporaneous coverage attributes this to market conditions and to questions about the durability of the business model. The offering prices at $40 on 37.5 million shares, raising approximately $1.5 billion. NVIDIA commits approximately $250 million as an anchor at the offer price. The price is cut by about 27 per cent and the size by about 23 per cent against the announced terms.

28 March 2025. Filed and reported. Shares list on Nasdaq under CRWV. The first trade is at $39, below the offer price.

6. April to December 2025: the run, the deal, and the argument

1 April 2025. Reported. Shares rise nearly 42 per cent, moving above the offer price for the first time.

20 June 2025. Reported. The stock closes at $183.58, its highest close, against a $40 offer price roughly twelve weeks earlier.

July 2025. Filed. CoreWeave agrees to acquire Core Scientific in an all-stock transaction valued at approximately $9 billion, at an exchange ratio of 0.1235 CoreWeave Class A shares per Core Scientific share. It is the second approach, following the rejected cash offer of June 2024.

September 2025. Reported. NVIDIA agrees to purchase CoreWeave capacity that goes unsold, in an arrangement running to April 2032 and valued at up to $6.3 billion. The supplier, investor, and second-largest customer becomes the buyer of last resort. This is the most significant change to the concentration position identified at the offering.

September 2025. Reported. Kerrisdale Capital publishes a short thesis describing the company as an undifferentiated and heavily levered GPU rental business, and putting fair value at $10, roughly 90 per cent below the price at the time. Its central accounting argument is that applying Nebius's four-year depreciation treatment takes CoreWeave's operating margin to approximately zero. It also characterises the asset-backed debt as carrying rates of 11 to 15 per cent, and puts Microsoft at roughly 70 per cent of revenue in the then-current quarter.

30 October 2025. Filed. Core Scientific shareholders vote against the transaction and the merger agreement is terminated. Two Seas Capital, describing itself as the largest active shareholder, had opposed it on process, structure, and valuation. Institutional Shareholder Services had recommended against. Core Scientific continues as an independent public company.

November 2025. Reported. Michael Burry argues that hyperscalers understate depreciation by assigning five- and six-year lives to hardware whose economic cycle is closer to two or three years, characterising the practice as one of the more common frauds of the modern era. He estimates the effect at $176 billion of suppressed depreciation expense across the group between 2026 and 2028, and calculates that it would inflate Oracle's 2028 earnings by 26.9 per cent and Meta's by 20.8 per cent. The companies named are Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and Oracle.

14 November 2025. Reported. Intrator responds publicly, saying the company is being data driven about the question. He states that CoreWeave's A100 fleet is fully booked, and that a batch of 2022-vintage H100s that came free when a contract expired was rebooked immediately at 95 per cent of the original price. Whether that is representative of the fleet is not established.

7. 2026: cheaper debt, and a lower price

March 2026. Filed. CoreWeave closes an $8.5 billion delayed draw term loan facility, DDTL 4.0, with a floating tranche priced at SOFR plus 2.25 per cent and a fixed tranche at approximately 5.9 per cent. Blackstone Credit and Insurance anchors it. MUFG and Morgan Stanley act as co-structuring agents and joint bookrunners, with Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan as additional coordinating lead arrangers. The company describes it as the first investment-grade rated GPU-backed financing. The pricing is substantially below the prior facility, which was at SOFR plus 4.00 per cent.

30 June 2026. Reported. Principal debt outstanding of approximately $35.6 billion, against $12.9 billion of commitments raised through the end of 2024.

2025 to 2026. Reported. Backlog diversifies beyond Microsoft, with reported commitments from OpenAI of approximately $22.4 billion, Meta at an implied $35.2 billion, and additional contracts with Anthropic and Jane Street. Microsoft is reported at approximately 67 per cent of fiscal 2025 revenue. These figures come from analyst compilations rather than filings and should be verified.

3 September 2026. Reported. The stock closes at $84.56, against a trailing 52-week range of $60.55 to $153.20 and a one-year change of about negative 31 per cent. It remains above the $40 offer price and roughly 54 per cent below the June 2025 high.

The open question. Whether CoreWeave has revised the six-year estimate since the offering. This research established neither that it has nor that it has not. It is answerable from the most recent annual report and is the first item to check.

Reported, and soft. These indices are commercial products, are not standardised, and blend on-demand with committed pricing. They establish a direction and a rough magnitude and will not support a precise claim.

Reported. A100 units, now roughly six years old, still transact on the secondary market at approximately $7,800 to $18,900 depending on memory and condition. H100 secondary pricing has been volatile, with units that reached about $50,000 at peak scarcity later selling at steep discounts.

Reported, contested. Analysis circulated in 2024 placed the useful life of data centre GPUs running AI workloads at 60 to 70 per cent utilisation at one to three years. NVIDIA ships a new architecture roughly every 18 to 24 months. The one-to-three figure is widely repeated by parties holding positions, and this research did not locate the underlying study. Do not put it in a student packet without finding it.

11. Where the record is thin

The 2023 change from five years to six is reported rather than confirmed here. It is the load-bearing fact of the whole chronology and it must be verified in the filing. If it is wrong, the sequence of events still holds but its centre moves.

The rental price series is directional only. The indices are commercial, are not standardised, and blend pricing types. Use the shape of the curve and not any single point.

The one-to-three-year economic life figure has weak provenance. It circulates widely, it is repeated by parties with positions on both sides, and the underlying study was not located.

Kerrisdale is advocacy. Its Nebius arithmetic is checkable and probably right in direction. Its rate range and its $10 valuation are a short seller's position.

The 2025 and 2026 backlog figures come from analyst compilations. The OpenAI, Meta, Anthropic, and Jane Street numbers were not traced to filings during this research.
