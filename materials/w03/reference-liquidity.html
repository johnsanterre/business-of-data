Reference note

Liquidity, solvency, and runway

The definitions and the standard calculations, in one place.

Two tests, on different clocks

Solvency asks whether everything a firm owns is worth more than everything it owes. No deadline attaches to the question. It is answered by a valuation, it covers the whole balance sheet, and it is an opinion about the future, because most of what a lender owns is a promise that somebody else will pay later.

Liquidity asks whether the cash available on a particular day is at least the cash demanded on that day. It is answered by a calendar rather than a valuation, it covers only the near term, and it is a fact rather than an opinion, because by close of business the payment either cleared or it did not.

A firm can fail either test independently of the other. The two failures have different causes, different cures, and speeds that differ by orders of magnitude. Insolvency is cured by new capital, by retained earnings, or by creditors agreeing to take less, each of which takes months at minimum. Illiquidity is cured within hours by a credit line, a lender of last resort, or an asset sale, and if none of the three is available on the day the money is due there is no fourth option.

The forced sale connects them. Selling under time pressure means selling at a discount, the discount is a realised loss, and enough of it converts a solvent firm into an insolvent one. The conversion runs in one direction: illiquidity can manufacture insolvency in a fortnight, and insolvency can sit for years without manufacturing anything.

Duration

Duration is the average time until a holder receives their money, weighting each payment by the share of present value it carries. For a loan paying interest annually and returning principal at maturity, duration is a little less than the term, and the lower the coupon the closer it gets to the term itself.

Macaulay duration is that weighted average, expressed in years. Modified duration divides it by one plus the yield and is used as a price sensitivity: the percentage fall in price for a one percentage point rise in rates.

The estimate is systematically wrong in a known direction. For an ordinary loan it overstates the loss when rates rise and understates the gain when they fall, and the error grows with the size of the shock. That curvature is convexity, and it makes duration a reliable early warning and an unreliable precise forecast.

The duration gap is asset duration less liability duration. A gap of eight years means the firm has promised to return tomorrow money it has lent out for eight years. That is not a distortion of lending, it is the service being provided, and the firm is paid for carrying it.

Worked

A ten-year loan of one hundred, paying a five per cent annual coupon with principal at maturity, priced to yield five per cent.

  • Price 100.00, Macaulay duration 8.11 years, modified duration 7.72
  • Rates rise two points: duration predicts −15.4 per cent, the exact price is 85.95, a fall of −14.0 per cent
  • Rates rise three points: duration predicts −23.2 per cent, the exact price is 79.87, a fall of −20.1 per cent
  • Funded by demand deposits, duration about 0.003 years, the gap is 8.10 years

Deposit beta and behavioural life

Contractually a demand deposit has a duration of about one day. Behaviourally, deposit balances are far stickier, so firms assign a modelled behavioural life, commonly three or four years. Using the contractual figure would overstate the risk so severely that the firm could not operate, so the modelling is not a dodge.

Deposit beta is the fraction of a rise in market rates that must be passed on to retain the balances. A beta of three tenths means a two point rise costs six tenths of a point in funding.

Two properties of the parameter matter more than its value. It is the only quantity in the calculation estimated from history rather than read off a contract. And it is not a constant but a function of the alternative available to the depositor, close to flat while no alternative exists and close to vertical once one does. A beta fitted across a period of near-zero rates was fitted where the parameter was never tested.

Runway

Gross burn is total cash out. Net burn is gross burn less cash in. Runway is unrestricted cash divided by net burn, expressed in months.

That figure is a cash-out date. The date a firm needs is its decision date, which is the cash-out date less the time required to change the situation. A financing runs about seven months from first serious conversation to money received, so a firm with 10.7 months of runway has a decision date at month 3.7 and roughly a third of the stated runway available for deciding anything.

Three assumptions sit inside the single number, and they are listed here in order of how much control the firm has over each.

  • Burn holds. It is the output of commitments made three to six months earlier, so reducing it takes about as long as creating it did, and a cut produces redundancy cost immediately against a saving that arrives later.
  • Revenue arrives. Revenue enters through the denominator, so a small revenue line attached to a large burn has an outsized effect on the date.
  • A next round exists. A property of the outside world, which no amount of good execution makes true.

The three are positively correlated, so stressing one at a time understates the risk. Compute the runway from unconditional cash alone, and show conditional money as a separate line carrying the date each condition is tested. A committed facility with conditions precedent binds when the firm needs it and is satisfied comfortably when it does not.

Worked

Cash of 48 million, gross burn 6.0 a month, revenue 1.5 a month.

  • Net burn 4.5 a month, runway 10.7 months
  • Decision date month 3.7, which is 34 per cent of the stated figure
  • Gross burn twenty per cent over plan: runway 8.4 months, 2.2 months lost
  • Revenue to zero: runway 8.0 months, 2.7 months lost

Concentration

The Herfindahl index is the sum of squared exposure shares. Its reciprocal is the number of equal-sized exposures that would produce the same index, and is reported as the effective number of names. It measures the distribution of sizes, and does that correctly.

Diversification works because independent outcomes cancel, so the count of exposures is only half of what determines whether a book moves as one thing. For N equal exposures with common pairwise correlation rho, the variance of the average is the individual variance multiplied by the quantity rho plus one minus rho over N. The second term is the diversification being bought and falls away as N grows. The first term contains no N. Risk falls toward rho and no further.

Setting that equal to the independent case gives an effective independent count of one divided by the same bracket, directly comparable to the reciprocal Herfindahl figure.

Worked

A book of four hundred equal loans.

  • rho = 0 → 400 effective loans, matching Herfindahl exactly
  • rho = 0.05 → 19.1
  • rho = 0.10 → 9.8
  • rho = 0.25 → 3.97, against a Herfindahl reading of 400 for the same book
  • A five-loan book of 40, 25, 15, 10, 10 per cent scores 3.8 on Herfindahl

Stated as volatility, four hundred independent loans swing five per cent as much as a single borrower. At correlation of a quarter the same book swings 50.2 per cent as much, which is ten times the volatility of the book a size-based measure describes.

Correlation is estimated rather than observed, and it is estimated from history. In calm periods the linking mechanism is dormant and the measured figure is low, so the estimate is at its most reassuring when it is least reliable. This is the same structural problem as deposit beta.

What each measure cannot see

  • A solvency test compares two figures at an instant and has no time axis, so it cannot show a duration gap however often it is run.
  • Herfindahl takes sizes as its input and has nowhere to record a common cause, so two books with the same effective exposure can differ by a factor of a hundred in the report.
  • Large exposure limits are written per counterparty, and the connected-client rules that extend them cover ownership, control, and guarantees. Those are legal relationships. A shared city, a shared dominant employer, and a shared refinancing market are not among them.
  • A runway figure answers when the cash reaches zero, and unless somebody performs the subtraction the month the firm's options close does not appear.
