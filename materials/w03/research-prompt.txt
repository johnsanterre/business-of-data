Produce an extensive, source-cited case study of Stability AI suitable for
teaching in a graduate business course, set against the other companies that
adopted the same open-weights posture. The teaching question is how a company
can be worth more than it owes and still fail: because its funding matures
sooner than the obligations it supports, because its assets cannot be turned
into cash on the timescale its creditors require, and because exposures that
look spread across many parties turn out to respond to a single event. Freeze
the narrative at the resignation of Emad Mostaque as chief executive on 22 March
2024. Material after that date belongs in a clearly separated final section.

Stability AI is a private company, and that changes what evidence exists. There
is no registration statement, no quarterly reporting, and no audited disclosure
of the kind a listed company must produce. Work first from the documents that do
exist. Those are the statutory filings of Stability AI Ltd and any associated
entity at Companies House in the United Kingdom, including annual accounts and
the notes to them, confirmation statements, statements of capital, share
allotment returns, the register of charges, and the appointment and termination
of directors; and the pleadings, exhibits, declarations, expert reports and
judgments in the litigation to which the company is a party. Give the company
number, the filing date, and the document type for anything drawn from Companies
House. A statutory account is a document. A press report describing a document
is not the same thing. A report quoting an internal deck the outlet obtained is
a third thing again, and it is the category most of this record falls into.

Mark every material claim with one of four labels, and use them consistently
rather than only where they are convenient.

  FILED           It appears in a statutory filing or a court document. Name
                  the document and the date.
  REPORTED        A named outlet, a named journalist, and a date. Give all
                  three. If the outlet says it obtained internal documents,
                  say so, and say whether the documents were published.
  ASSERTED        A party to a dispute, a former employee, or the company
                  itself says it. Attribute it and note the speaker's interest.
  NOT ESTABLISHED It circulates without a traceable source.

Where a figure is disputed, give both figures and say who asserts each. Where
something is not disclosed, say that it is not disclosed rather than estimating
around the gap. Throughout, separate what a person outside the company could
have known on a given date from what became knowable only later. That
separation is the point of the exercise, so date the availability of every
significant fact, not only the fact itself.

Several figures recur in the reporting on this company. Treat each as a lead to
be established rather than as a fact: check it against a filing or against the
original reporting, give the source and the date, and say plainly if it is
unsupported, misquoted, or wrong. This applies to the revenue and expense
figures for 2023, the amount owed under the compute contracts, the size and
terms of the October 2022 round, and the valuation.

Cover the following.

1. Formation, entities, and ownership. The founding, the date, and the corporate
   structure: the United Kingdom entity, any United States entity, and how they
   relate. Directors and officers with their appointment and termination dates
   from the statutory record. The share classes, the allotments, and what the
   capital structure looked like at each raise. The dispute over founding
   equity, what each side claimed, and how it resolved. Where the founding
   narrative the company told publicly differs from what the filings and the
   litigation show, set the two side by side.

2. What the company released, and who built it. Stable Diffusion 1.x, 2.x, SDXL,
   SD3, and the audio and language models. For each, establish who did the
   research, which institution employed them, who supplied the training data,
   who supplied the compute, and who held whatever rights existed. Distinguish
   what Stability built from what Stability funded, convened, or hosted. This
   distinction is load-bearing for the case: a company can be the reason
   something happened without owning the thing that happened.

3. The financial record. Revenue by year and, if the record permits, by line of
   business. Cost of revenue. Compute spend. Personnel cost and headcount over
   time. Cash held at each date the record gives one. Monthly burn. Losses.
   Take everything possible from the Companies House accounts, quoting the note
   where the figure appears; label everything else REPORTED with the outlet and
   the date. State explicitly which of these figures were never disclosed in any
   form, and which appeared only through documents an outlet obtained.

4. The funding stack, instrument by instrument. Every raise: the date, the
   amount, the instrument, the pre-money and post-money valuation, the lead
   investor, and the other participants. Distinguish priced equity from
   convertible notes and from simple agreements for future equity. For every
   convertible instrument, give the maturity date, the conversion trigger, the
   discount, the cap, and any liquidation preference, to the extent the record
   discloses them. Note where terms are not disclosed, which will be often. The
   maturity profile of the funding is the first half of the duration question,
   so assemble it as a timeline rather than as a list.

5. The compute contracts and the obligations they created. Every arrangement
   with a compute supplier by name: the counterparty, the date, the size, the
   term, whether it carried a minimum commitment or a take-or-pay provision,
   and what was owed and when. Establish what was disputed, what went unpaid,
   and what was renegotiated. Then set the tenor of these commitments against
   the tenor of the funding assembled in section four, and state the mismatch in
   months. Include any lease, hardware purchase, or hosting commitment that
   functions as a liability. Note where an obligation would not appear as debt
   on a balance sheet.

6. The runway calculation, reconstructed at several dates. Choose at least four
   dates between the first large raise and the freeze date. At each one, state
   cash on hand, monthly burn, and months of runway remaining, showing the
   arithmetic and naming the source of every input. Then say which of the three
   inputs is least well supported at that date, and what the calculation assumes
   about the availability of the next round. Where the inputs do not exist for a
   date, say so and give the nearest date where they do.

7. Concentration, in four forms. Revenue concentration by customer, to the
   extent the record shows it. Investor concentration, and whether the parties
   funding successive rounds were independent of one another or responsive to
   the same signals. Supplier concentration in compute. Key-person concentration
   in the chief executive, including how much of the fundraising, the public
   profile, and the counterparty relationships ran through one individual. For
   each, state what would have to happen for the exposure to move as a single
   position rather than as several.

8. The departures, examined as a run. Assemble a dated sequence of the
   researcher and executive exits: the authors of the diffusion work, the
   finance and operating executives, the board members, and the chief executive.
   For each, give the date, the destination where known, and the stated reason.
   Then establish the order: did the exits precede the public reporting on the
   company's finances, follow it, or interleave with it. Determine whether the
   people leaving were the ones who could see the numbers. A run is a
   coordination problem in which acting becomes rational for each party once it
   becomes likely that others will act, so test whether that structure fits what
   happened here, and say where it does not fit.

9. Warning indicators, and who held them. Establish who inside the company could
   see the burn and the runway at each stage, when the board was told, what the
   board did, and what the company said publicly in the same weeks. Identify any
   point where a person who could see the problem had no authority to act on it,
   and any point where a person with authority was not shown the numbers.
   Distinguish what was concealed from what was disclosed to a small group from
   what was simply not calculated.

10. Open-weights companies: funding raised against revenue. This is the
    comparative core of the report, and the reading it supports is named for it.
    Cover Stability AI, Mistral AI, Hugging Face, Black Forest Labs, Runway,
    Midjourney, Cohere, and Meta's Llama programme, adding any other company
    that adopted a comparable release posture. For each, give total capital
    raised with dates, the most recent reported valuation, reported revenue with
    its date and source, headcount, the monetisation route actually used, and
    the compute posture, meaning owned, leased, committed, or provided by a
    partner. Midjourney and Meta are the two ends of the range and both belong
    in the table: one reached the same market with no outside capital, and the
    other released weights while selling nothing, funded by an unrelated
    business. Present this as a table, and follow it with an analysis of which
    of these companies faced the same duration mismatch and which did not, and
    what structural feature explains the difference. Where a private company's
    revenue is not established, say so in the table rather than carrying an
    estimate into the comparison.

11. The bear case as it stood in early 2024, constructed only from what was
    knowable then. State which assumptions in the company's plan carried the
    most weight with the least support, what would have falsified each one, and
    what an investor, a compute supplier, and a staff researcher could each have
    observed from their own position. Keep this section strictly inside the
    information available before 22 March 2024.

12. A separate final section, clearly labelled as after the decision point. The
    change in control and the recapitalisation, the terms to the extent
    disclosed, the new leadership and board, the treatment of existing
    investors, the restructuring and any change in headcount, the resolution or
    continuation of the compute obligations, the outcome of the litigation in
    each jurisdiction, subsequent filings at Companies House including later
    accounts, and the position of the company at the time of writing. Also
    record what happened to the researchers who left and to the companies they
    founded.

Deliver as a structured report with numbered sections. Include a table of every
quantitative claim carrying its source, its date, and its label from the four
above. Include a timeline table of funding instruments showing amount, date,
instrument type, and maturity or conversion trigger where known, so the funding
profile can be read against the obligation profile. Include the comparative
table from section ten. Include a bibliography with full citations, separating
statutory and court documents from journalism from company statements.

Close with two short lists. First, the places where the public record is thin
enough that a reader should not rely on it. Second, the questions this record
cannot answer at all, and what document would be required to answer each. For a
private company the second list is part of the finding, not an apology for it.
