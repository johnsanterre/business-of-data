Course mechanics

Description, format, and assignments

Description

What the course covers

This course examines the decisions that determine whether a model becomes a product: how assets are valued and financed, how models are validated and governed, what may lawfully enter a training corpus, how work is contracted outside the firm, how products are priced and withdrawn, and how firms respond to litigation and regulation. Most of these decisions are made by people who did not build the model.

Instruction is by case study. Students work through twelve companies and the decisions their managers faced, using the documentary record that was available at the time. There is no programming component.

Format

Structure of a week

Each week consists of a 90-minute asynchronous module and a 90-minute synchronous session. The asynchronous module covers one business function and does not refer to the week's case. It must be completed before the synchronous session.

The synchronous session is a case discussion. Four students are assigned roles for the week and are responsible for presenting and defending the decisions taken by the parties they represent. The remaining students submit three written questions before the session and are expected to participate in the discussion.

Two versions of each case are distributed five days before the session. The version issued to the class ends at the decision point. The version issued to the four assigned students includes the outcome and what followed. The class therefore does not know the answer, and the outcome emerges through questioning rather than through an announcement at the end.

Students account for the decision that was taken rather than proposing an alternative. Hindsight makes most of these decisions look obvious, and a student asked what they would have done can answer without understanding the constraints the decision was made under. Reconstructing and defending the actual decision requires establishing those constraints first.

Questions go to the instructor rather than to the assigned students. A question is assessed on whether it requires a party to state an assumption, rather than on whether it has a correct answer.

Rotation

Role assignment

A section of sixteen students fills four roles a week across twelve case weeks, which is forty-eight assignments and three turns for each student.

The schedule gives every student three different roles and one appearance in each third of the semester. No student is assigned alongside the same classmate more than once, so each works with nine different colleagues over the three sessions.

Final project

Writing a teaching case

Groups of four produce a case from a company one member has access to: a decision a member observed, a current or former employer, or an industry a member can obtain documents from.

Access matters more than how well known the company is. A small decision supported by real documents produces a better case than a prominent one supported only by news coverage. Four members means four possible sources of material, which is the reason this is assigned to groups.

Anonymize by default. A description such as "a mid-size health insurer" serves the case as well as the name does. Named employers require written permission from someone with authority to give it, included in the appendix. Material under a non-disclosure agreement may not be used, whether anonymized or not. Synchronous sessions are recorded.

Objectives

Learning objectives

On completion of the course, students will be able to:

  • Identify the business functions involved in releasing a data product, and the information available to each of them.
  • Read the primary financial and legal documents that record these decisions, including registration statements, annual reports, and court filings.
  • Apply model risk management and validation frameworks to a deployed model.
  • Reconstruct a management decision from the information available at the time, and defend it under questioning.
  • Write a teaching case with exhibits, from primary sources.
